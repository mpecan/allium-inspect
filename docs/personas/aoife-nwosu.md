# Aoife Nwosu — the new joiner

> "There are six thousand lines of this and I do not know what any of it is."

Aoife joined the team on Monday. She is a competent engineer, has never seen
Allium, and has been told that the spec is the source of truth and she should
start there. It is Tuesday. Nobody has time to walk her through it.

She is the persona who reads the interface itself rather than the spec, because
she has no other way in. Every label, subtitle and empty state is load-bearing
for her, and she is the only one who will notice when one of them is missing.

## What she comes for

1. **A way in that is not the top of a file.** Six thousand lines with no
   entry point is not a specification, it is a wall. She needs somewhere the
   tool says "start here".
2. **Vocabulary.** What is an entity here, what is a surface, what is a trigger,
   how do they relate. She will learn this from what the tool calls things and
   from what it puts next to what.
3. **One thread, followed.** Pick something — a message — and see everything
   about it: what it holds, what changes it, what it leads to. Learning a system
   is learning one thing properly and then generalising.
4. **The shape of a module.** Which of the five is the core and which are
   supporting? She would get this in five minutes from a good picture and never
   from a directory listing.

## What she knows, and what she does not

She knows how to read code and how to learn a system. She is not confused by a
graph.

She does not know a single construct name in this spec set. Search only helps
her if she can search by *kind* — "show me the surfaces" — because she has no
names yet.

She does not know the difference between what Allium models and what this tool
adds. She will take the tool's structure as the language's structure, which
means anything the tool invents becomes something she believes about Allium.

## What good looks like

- The first screen tells her what she is looking at and what she can do.
- Every choice is labelled with the question it answers, not with its own name.
- She can go from "no idea" to "I understand messaging" in twenty minutes with
  nobody helping.
- Nothing is discoverable only by hovering or by knowing a keyboard shortcut.

## What would make her stop

- **It assumes she has a name to type.** An empty search box over a spec she
  cannot name anything in is a dead end.
- **Vocabulary with no definition anywhere.** "Trigger", "surface", "obligation"
  used as if she knew them, in a tool whose whole purpose is explaining a spec.
- **A default view that is a hairball.** She cannot tell whether that is the
  spec's complexity or the tool's failure, and either way she has learned
  nothing.

## The checks she settles

- Open the tool on a spec set you do not know. Without touching the source, what
  can you learn in ten minutes, and what did you have to guess?
- Try to find "all the surfaces" with no names in your head. Does search take a
  kind? Is there another way?
- Read the empty states. Do they say what to do, or only that there is nothing?
- Count the terms used without definition anywhere in the interface. Every one
  is a question she will have to ask a colleague.
- Take the default landing view. Does it orient or does it overwhelm?
